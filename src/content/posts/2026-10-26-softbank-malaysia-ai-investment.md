Malaysian Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim said on Saturday, October 24, that SoftBank Group founder Masayoshi Son is considering an investment of up to $50 billion in the country. The focus would be AI, new technology and data centers. [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/26/softbank-masayoshi-son-malaysia-ai-billions.html) reported that Anwar, citing a Bloomberg report, said Son had identified sites in Kuching, Sarawak, and in peninsular Malaysia after a video call between the two.

## What is and is not confirmed

The figure and the sites come from the prime minister. CNBC said SoftBank did not immediately respond to a request for comment, so the company has not confirmed the plan. [The New Straits Times](https://www.nst.com.my/news/nation/2026/10/1563061/global-investor-plans-us50bil-investment-malaysias-ai-data-centre) and [The Japan Times](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/business/2026/10/26/softbank-malaysia-data-center-investments/) also reported Anwar's remarks.

Earlier this month, according to CNBC, SoftBank signed a framework agreement with Grab and Petroleum Sarawak Berhad. Malaysia has said it wants to become an "AI nation" by 2030. Its investment agency, MIDA, reports RM385.7 billion (about $94 billion) of data-center investment from 2021 to the first half of 2026.

## Why it matters (analysis)

Until SoftBank confirms the figure, readers should treat it as a stated ambition rather than a commitment. Still, it fits a pattern of governments competing for [AI infrastructure](/topics/ai-infrastructure/) money, and Sarawak's energy resources are a likely draw.
