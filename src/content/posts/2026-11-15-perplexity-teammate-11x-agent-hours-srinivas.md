Perplexity chief executive Aravind Srinivas has put a new number on how much work the company's own staff are handing to its Teammate agents. In a [post on X](https://x.com/AravSrinivas/status/2121803009432953255) late on Saturday 14 November (US Eastern time), he wrote that it is now "11x more agent hours per week from Teammate".

## What he claimed

Srinivas said the figure is growing by more than 50% week over week and compared it with 7x a week earlier. That earlier figure came from [his 6 November post](https://x.com/AravSrinivas/status/2118549314746429552), in which he said the median Perplexity employee was getting 7 times more agent hours per week from Teammates than in September, and that one in five staff were already at 40 or more agent hours a week.

In the new post he also said the number of employees getting an extra workday (8 or more agent hours) or an extra work week (40 or more hours) from Teammate has more than tripled. He put the value of that work at roughly $80 million to $180 million a year in labour against about $10 million in tokens, or 9 to 19 times the cost depending on the job.

## What has not been confirmed

These are internal figures shared by the company's chief executive. We could not find a Perplexity blog post, filing or other page that publishes the underlying data, the baseline used for "September", or how agent hours and labour value are measured. Treat them as Srinivas's claims rather than independently verified results.

## Background

Perplexity launched Teammates, personal work agents that run tasks on a user's behalf, on 5 November. Two days later Srinivas said automation runs had grown 3.7 times from launch day to day two, [as we reported](https://aiheadlinehub.com/2026/11/08/perplexity-teammates-automation-runs-3-7x/).

## Our read

Analysis: the numbers describe Perplexity's own employees, a group that is likely to adopt the product faster than outside customers. They show how the company wants Teammate to be judged, in hours of work and cost per hour, but they are not yet evidence of how the agents perform for paying users.
