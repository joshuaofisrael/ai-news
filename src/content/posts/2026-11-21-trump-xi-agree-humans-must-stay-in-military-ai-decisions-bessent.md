The US and China are racing each other on AI, but their leaders have agreed on one limit. "TRUMP AND XI AGREE AGAINST FULLY AUTONOMOUS MILITARY AI DECISION-MAKING, BESSENT SAYS," [First Squawk posted on X](https://x.com/FirstSquawk/status/2124089682506240064) on Saturday, citing the South China Morning Post.

## What Bessent said

The claim comes from Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, who travelled with President Donald Trump to Asia. In an interview on CNBC's Squawk on the Street on Friday, according to the [CNBC transcript](https://pressroom.versantmedia.com/cnbc/press-releases/cnbc-exclusive-transcript-us-treasury-secretary-scott-bessent-speaks-cnbcs-1), he called it one of the trip's headline results: "I think it was very important that the president, President Trump, and President Xi agreed that humans should always be involved in any military decisions that SI make, makes, and the SI should not be allowed to be autonomous for major military actions."

SI stands for "super intelligence", the Trump administration's preferred term for AI, [Reuters explains](https://www.militarytimes.com/news/pentagon-congress/2026/11/20/trump-xi-agreed-that-military-ai-decisions-should-involve-humans-bessent-says/). The two leaders met in Shenzhen on 19 November during the APEC summit.

## What China's readout says

Beijing's own account is less specific. China's [foreign ministry readout](https://www.mfa.gov.cn/eng/xw/zyxw/202611/t20261119_12063139.html) says the two countries "should strengthen communication and coordination, prevent the security risks of AI, and ensure that AI is always under human control." It does not mention military operations directly. No joint text of the agreement has been published.

## Rivals, not partners

Bessent was clear that the deal does not soften the competition. "One is the SI race, and obviously the, the U.S. is ahead. We must stay ahead," he told CNBC. He said Chinese models "are dangerous" and that "any SI-based attack that you see comes from these, the Chinese models." On the military pledge, though: "there's nothing to do with competition when it says that both sides agree that no major military operation should be launched without a human being involved."

## Why it matters

Keeping a human "in the loop" for lethal decisions is one of the oldest asks in AI safety, and it is the first time Washington has described a Trump-Xi agreement on it. But this is a verbal understanding reported by one side, with no published wording, no definition of a "major" action and no way to check compliance. The thing to watch is whether it turns into a written statement, or shows up in the action plan both sides said they would draft.
