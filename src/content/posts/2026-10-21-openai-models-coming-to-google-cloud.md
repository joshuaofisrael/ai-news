OpenAI said on October 21, 2026 that it is partnering with Google Cloud to bring OpenAI’s frontier models and Codex agentic workflows into Google Cloud’s Model Garden. The [announcement](https://openai.com/index/openai-models-google-cloud) is the source for the claims below.

The post says customers will get native access to those models for agentic workflows, using Google Cloud’s data governance and infrastructure. For regulated industries, OpenAI says deploying the models means deciding who can reach sensitive data and how that data is used. It describes the integration as pairing the models with Google Cloud’s access controls, centralized data governance, and high-performance infrastructure.

OpenAI says customers will be able to purchase the frontier models in Model Garden, with the sale running through the Google Cloud Marketplace. Enterprises that already have Google Cloud spend commitments will be able to draw those commitments down for the purchases, the post says.

The same announcement says the models will soon be available in Google Antigravity and the Gemini Agent, which OpenAI presents as a way for enterprise customers to choose a model for a task. The post does not describe what those two products include beyond that.

General availability is planned in the coming weeks. The announcement does not give a calendar date. A separate [preview page](https://openai.com/form/openai-on-google-cloud/) says a limited preview is open by invitation. It lists two pieces: frontier models through the Responses API on Google Cloud, for supported development and internal testing, and Codex through the API in the Codex app, the command-line interface, and supported development environments. OpenAI’s wording is that the preview is invite-only and that registering is a request to be considered.

Greg Brockman, OpenAI’s president and co-founder, says in the announcement that Google Cloud is “a leading platform for developers and businesses building AI agents,” and that the partnership is “an important step in making our most capable models and Codex available where customers are already building.” Thomas Kurian, Google Cloud’s chief executive, says the company wants “a broad selection of models, including those from OpenAI,” so customers can “choose the right model for any given job.”

The same post draws a boundary around the deal. Microsoft remains OpenAI’s primary cloud partner.

The same day, OpenAI published a separate note on [partner safeguards and updated usage policies](/2026/10/21/openai-partner-safeguards-and-usage-policies/). That note is about how safety rules travel when other companies host or resell the models. Neither that note nor the Google Cloud posts list Model Garden prices or the model names that will be sold there.
