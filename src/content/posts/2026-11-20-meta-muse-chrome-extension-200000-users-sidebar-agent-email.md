Meta's AI agent already had a crowd in Chrome before Meta said a word. "Belated Muse feature alert," Meta's chief AI officer Alexandr Wang [posted on X](https://x.com/alexandr_wang/status/2123591819300934128) late on Thursday. "Muse chrome extension, available now! 200,000 of you are already feeling the magic, but muse chrome extension lets you use muse directly from a chrome sidebar, and easier browser use!"

## What the extension does

[Digital Trends reports](https://www.digitaltrends.com/computing/meta-is-putting-muse-in-a-chrome-sidebar-and-the-official-announcement-just-arrived/) that the "Muse from Meta" listing on the Chrome Web Store shows 200,000 users and a 4.6 rating, and that TestingCatalog first spotted it on 13 November. The extension puts Muse in Chrome's side panel so you can talk to it from any page.

When Muse acts, it uses your own Chrome. Digital Trends says it opens its tabs in a separate group and can only act inside those tabs. Meta says it cannot see saved passwords, browsing history or Chrome settings, and passwords on a page are removed before Muse reads it. You can approve actions before they happen, check an activity log and take control back by clicking the page, pressing Esc or dragging a tab out of the group. It needs Chrome 137 or later.

## The catch

Muse can use any site you are already logged into. Meta also warns that the agent may take unexpected actions and that its activity may show up in your Google account and browsing history, which could affect the ads you see. Digital Trends suggests logging out of any account you don't want the agent touching before connecting it.

## Email too

Wang added a second update about 20 minutes later: "email is now rolling out to everyone in supported markets," he [wrote](https://x.com/alexandr_wang/status/2123596935231856931), inviting users to pick a muse.ai email handle. He did not list the markets.

## Why it matters

Browser agents are the new battleground. Running inside a user's own logged-in browser gets around much of the friction that stops agents from booking or buying things, but it also raises the stakes when they get something wrong. Muse has been growing quickly; our earlier story covers [Muse Spaces and custom mini-apps](/2026/11/08/meta-muse-spaces-custom-mini-apps/).
