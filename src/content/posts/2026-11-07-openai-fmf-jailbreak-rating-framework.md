OpenAI [announced on X](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2118795218099253606) on 6 November 2026 that it worked with the Frontier Model Forum and member firms on a framework for rating cyber jailbreaks.

> "We partnered with @fmf_org and its member firms on a new framework to help identify when bypassing an AI system's safeguards could enable serious cyber harm, and prioritize stronger protections."
>
> — OpenAI (@OpenAI), [6 November 2026](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2118795218099253606)

The longer note quotes Sasha Baker, OpenAI's head of national security policy, calling the work a step toward a common methodology for comparing the severity of cyber jailbreaks. The [Frontier Model Forum update](https://www.frontiermodelforum.org/updates/introducing-the-fmf-jailbreak-rating-framework-for-frontier-cyber-threats/) describes Version 1.0 of the Jailbreak Rating Framework for Frontier Cyber Threats.

## Shared rating, not a new model

This is a standards and policy story, not a product launch. OpenAI frames the framework as a way for AI and security teams to build a clearer evidence base and prioritize safeguard work. Severity labels and scoring dimensions come from the Forum's published specification; they are industry self-definitions, not a regulator mandate.

The public anchors remain the [OpenAI X post](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2118795218099253606) and the [FMF announcement](https://www.frontiermodelforum.org/updates/introducing-the-fmf-jailbreak-rating-framework-for-frontier-cyber-threats/).
