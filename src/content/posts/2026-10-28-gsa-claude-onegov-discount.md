The U.S. General Services Administration said on October 27, 2026 that it has expanded its OneGov agreement with Anthropic. The [GSA release](https://www.gsa.gov/about-gsa/newsroom/news-releases/gsa-anthropic-reach-onegov-agreement-on-discounted-consumptionbased-pricing-10272026) says participating governments get a 40 percent discount on token-based use of Claude models available to the government, including FedRAMP-authorized environments for sensitive unclassified work. There is no minimum order. Agencies pay for what they use. The price cut is a procurement term. It is not a recommendation to buy, and this report is not purchasing or legal advice.

## Who GSA says can order

GSA’s list is federal buyers in the executive, legislative, and judicial branches, plus state, local, and tribal governments. Ordering, it says, can be direct, through resellers, or on major cloud platforms. The release points agencies to ordering details at itvmo.gsa.gov/onegov. Its subheading calls the offering “Advanced Super Intelligence Tools.” That phrase uses “Super Intelligence,” the label set out in Executive Order 14434 and covered in [the terminology file](/2026/10/28/super-intelligence-document-term/).

GSA gives a savings figure for the OneGov program as a whole, not for this discount alone. It says OneGov has generated more than $1.8 billion in savings for the federal government since it began, including about $1.6 billion through its super intelligence agreements. It says that as offers expire, it will renew agreements where appropriate.

## What Anthropic adds

Anthropic’s [post the same day](https://www.anthropic.com/news/expanding-partnership-gsa) names the discounted products as Claude Enterprise, Claude for Government, and the Claude API. It says this is the first time the discount covers every product surface: direct purchases now, and purchases through cloud providers in the coming months. Anthropic’s eligibility list adds territorial governments to the federal, state, local, and tribal buyers. It says agencies can buy at the OneGov discount through GSA’s Multiple Award Schedule.

Anthropic estimates that the discounts will save the government $1 billion. That sentence is Anthropic’s estimate. It is a different number from GSA’s $1.6 billion, which GSA ties to super intelligence agreements across OneGov, not to this Claude discount by itself. Anthropic also says agencies have no minimum spend.

On use, Anthropic says about 500 U.S. federal, state, and local government organizations now use Claude. It says weekly active users of Claude for Government are up about eightfold since February, and usage across federal civilian agencies is up about twentyfold in a year. Those counts are Anthropic’s.

Anthropic says Claude for Government became generally available on September 30 and is authorized at FedRAMP High, with coding and agent features close to the commercial product. It says agencies can call Claude on Amazon Bedrock or Google Cloud Vertex AI. On Bedrock, it says Claude is authorized for FedRAMP High, and selected models are authorized for Defense Department IL4 and IL5 workloads in AWS GovCloud. On Vertex AI, it says Claude is authorized for FedRAMP High. Claude Enterprise, it says, is in process for FedRAMP Moderate through the FedRAMP 20x program. GSA’s release mentions FedRAMP-authorized environments and does not repeat those High, Moderate, IL4, or IL5 labels.

Anthropic also says it is starting a program to help agencies put Claude to use, and that the practices it uses with private customers will reach public-sector buyers in the coming weeks. The post includes short accounts of agency work, including a state pilot and security tasks. Those accounts are Anthropic’s examples. This desk does not have the underlying project records.

## Why it matters (analysis)

The shift in the two posts is from a seat-style offer toward tokens: a stated 40 percent cut, no minimum, and a wider set of governments. The documents do not publish the undiscounted token price, so the dollar cost of a given workload is not fixed by the announcements. GSA’s $1.6 billion and Anthropic’s $1 billion answer different questions, and only Anthropic attaches its figure to this discount. The same day’s GSA wording, “Super Intelligence,” is the terminology in the September order, written up separately. Files on the company are on the [Anthropic](/topics/anthropic/) page, with other rules on the [AI policy](/topics/ai-policy/) page.
