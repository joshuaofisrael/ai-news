Microsoft plans to let businesses take the weights of its own MAI models and run them inside their own Azure environments. The company introduced the offering, called MAI Licensed Weights, at its Ignite conference on Tuesday, in a [post from Microsoft AI](https://microsoft.ai/news/introducing-mai-licensed-weights/).

## What Microsoft is offering

Microsoft says organisations will be able to adapt MAI models with their own data and expertise and decide how the models are deployed, who can access them and when they are upgraded. "Your data and adaptations remain yours," the post says, with Microsoft using them to train its models only with the customer's "express agreement".

The offering is "coming soon" and will be sold through Microsoft's Frontier Company engagements; Microsoft tells interested companies to contact their account team. It has not published pricing, eligibility rules or licence terms.

## The customer results Microsoft cites

Microsoft points to three customers that adapted MAI models. It says AT&T's customised models beat GPT-6.1 Sol on standard 5G network analysis tasks at about 1/50th of the cost; Woven by Toyota got ready-to-merge code fixes in 44% of cases where its existing frontier models needed a human; and Alaska Airlines raised accuracy on classifying aircraft maintenance reports from 81% to 91%. Microsoft notes that these are customer-specific evaluations and that results vary by workload, model setup and testing method.

## Part of a bigger MAI push

The licensing plan came alongside a wider [MAI launch at Ignite](https://microsoft.ai/news/microsoft-ignite-2026/) in which Microsoft released seven models, including MAI Code 2, MAI Code 2 Flash, MAI Cyber 2 Flash and MAI Image 2.7, and said its second-generation Maia 200 AI chip is now in production running its largest models. Microsoft pitched the line-up on price per task, saying that "no one wants to pour their IQ into someone else's model".

## Our read

Analysis: licensed weights put Microsoft between closed, hosted models and open-weight downloads. Companies get more control without Microsoft giving the weights away. Whether it catches on will depend on the licence terms and prices, which Microsoft has not yet shared.
