Canada's federal government said on October 29, 2026 that it will make an equity investment of up to about C$1.6 billion in Cohere, the Toronto-founded AI company. The [Prime Minister's office](https://www.pm.gc.ca/en/news/news-releases/2026/10/29/canada-announces-landmark-investment-cohere-scale-secure-sovereign-ai) calls it a commercial investment, announced alongside private investors including NVIDIA, Bell, and Schwarz Group. The money will be paid in stages, the release says, with regular reporting on Cohere's progress. [CBC News](https://www.cbc.ca/news/business/canada-cohere-ai-investment-9.7401367) also reported the announcement.

## What Cohere agrees to do

According to the government release, Cohere commits to:

- create at least 1,000 new Canadian technical jobs by 2030;
- put at least C$1.7 billion into additional computing infrastructure in Canada by 2030;
- keep its Canadian incorporation and headquarters;
- fund research partnerships in Canada, with openings for students and recent graduates;
- keep working with the Canadian AI Safety Institute.

The release says that staging the payments against those commitments is meant to "hold the company accountable." It does not publish the size of each stage, the valuation, or the stake Canada will end up holding.

## What the government and Cohere said

Prime Minister Mark Carney framed the deal around control of data. "Canada's investment in Cohere is an investment in choice, in security, and in our ability to live our lives as we choose," he said in the release. Evan Solomon, the minister for AI and digital innovation, said the message is that founders "should not have to leave Canada to build a company that succeeds around the world." Cohere co-founder and chief executive Aidan Gomez said the money would help the company "attract world-class talent, build frontier AI systems, and develop secure, sovereign technology."

The release also points to Cohere's recent merger with Germany's Aleph Alpha, and says investment from NVIDIA and Schwarz Group in this round strengthens its international partnerships.

## Why it matters (analysis)

Governments have mostly backed AI through grants, compute programmes, or contracts. Taking an equity stake in a single model developer is a more direct bet, and it means taxpayers share in both gains and losses. The staged structure gives Ottawa some leverage, but the public release leaves out the terms that would let outsiders judge the price.
