Apple has launched a research app to study how people use AI to answer health questions, [TechCrunch reports](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/15/apple-launches-new-app-to-study-how-people-use-ai-for-health/). It is working with OpenEvidence, a healthcare-focused AI company.

## Who can join

The study is open to people in the US aged over 21 with an iPhone 15 or later running iOS 26 or later and Apple Intelligence switched on, according to TechCrunch. Apple aims to enrol up to 200,000 participants for more than 12 months. The app is available to download now in the US.

## How it works

Participants can ask health questions much as they would with a general chatbot, but the answers are grounded in clinical sources that include The New England Journal of Medicine, the Journal of the American Medical Association and Nature. Apple says the app will try to give simplified answers to everyday medical questions. Users can rate answers and will be asked to complete monthly surveys.

People can choose to share HealthKit data such as resting heart rate, activity, sleep or lab results for more personal suggestions. TechCrunch says the app can also help people prepare for a doctor's visit and will point users to providers when a question involves a concerning medical issue.

## Privacy

Apple told TechCrunch the data will be anonymised before it is sent to a secure third-party cloud provider and will not be used to train AI models. OpenEvidence deletes query and response data after 24 hours, the company said.

## Our read

Analysis: OpenAI and Anthropic already offer health features inside their chatbots. Apple is taking a slower route, running a formal study first. TechCrunch notes it is not yet clear whether the work will lead to AI answers in the Health app or Siri.
